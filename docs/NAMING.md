# Naming the game

Research from 30 September 2026. The working title is still Squares: nothing in the game or the repo has been renamed.

## The short answer

**Keep Goose at Large.** I checked 109 names, and nothing I found beats it. No game, app or trademark uses it, all three domains are available, it says the chase in plain words, and it works in a sentence.

The best new name is **Goose of Interest**. It's just as clean, and it would win if the mysteries ever became the main event. **Have You Seen This Goose?** is the most instantly understood, but you can't say it in a sentence, so use it as the tagline.

For the goose itself: **Geraldine**.

## How I checked

- **Brainstorm:** 109 names across the nine angles you asked for, plus your four favorites for comparison.
- **Domains:** the .com and .app for every name, checked two ways. First DNS (Python's `socket.getaddrinfo`, plus a direct lookup of each domain's name servers). Then a real registrar check through Vercel's domain API (read-only; nothing was bought). The two disagreed four times, always the same way: no DNS, but registered anyway (gatecrasher.com, lookit.app, anser.app, birdsofafeather.app). So "doesn't resolve" really does only mean "probably free". The tables use the registrar's answer.
- **.game:** every name came back unregistered in DNS, and the registrar confirmed it for the lead and the top names. But .game costs about $350 a year ([prices across registrars](https://tld-list.com/tld/game)), so I don't recommend buying one.
- **Stores:** web searches limited to apps.apple.com, play.google.com, store.steampowered.com and itch.io.
- **Trademarks:** web searches limited to Justia Trademarks and Trademarkia. The sites themselves are blocked from this computer, so I saw what a search engine shows: the mark, the owner, the number, and sometimes the goods. That's a first pass, not a legal clearance.
- **Who owns the search results:** a plain web search for each name.
- **Where the checks ran out:** the session had a cap of 200 web searches, and it ran out before every name was done. The table's "Checked" column says how far each name got. Six names never got their web searches: Stowaway Goose, Follow That Goose, Goose Noir, Goosescape, The Goose Is Afoot and Goose Afoot. Their domains are all available.
- Where a fact comes from a search engine's summary rather than a link I could see, it says "per the search summary".

## Goose at Large, stress-tested

**Verdict: keep it. Nothing I found beats it.**

### Why it holds up

- "At large" is what the police say about a suspect who hasn't been caught. Three words carry the premise (a goose on the run), the wanted posters, the Courier who can never find the addressee, and Inspector Pidge one place behind.
- It also means "out in the world", which covers everywhere from Plum Island to the Maasai Mara.
- It's adult without trying: police-blotter language, not a pun.
- It works in a sentence ("Have you played Goose at Large?"), it's spelled the way it sounds, and at 14 characters it leaves room for a subtitle.
- The icon draws itself: the goose on a wanted poster stamped AT LARGE.

### Evidence

- **App Store:** no app called Goose at Large. The search turns up other goose apps: [Goose Goose Duck](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/goose-goose-duck/id1558248707), [Goosechase](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/goosechase/id437330879), [Tingus Goose](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tingus-goose-weird-idle-game/id6444581718), [Goose Sounds](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/goose-sounds/id1070671545), [Goose Hunting Calls](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/goose-hunting-calls/id333249820).
- **Google Play:** none. The results: the 2006 film [Goose on the Loose](https://play.google.com/store/movies/details/Goose_on_the_Loose?id=62CB16DC0C69FEE9MV&hl=en_US) on Play Movies, [Goosechase](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.goosechaseadventures.goosechase), [Goose Goose Duck](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details/Goose_Goose_Duck?id=com.Gaggle.fun.GooseGooseDuck&hl=en_ZA), [GOOSE.IO](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.ammonite.goose&hl=en_IN).
- **Steam and itch.io:** none. The results: [Goose Goose Duck](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1568590/Goose_Goose_Duck/), [Mighty Goose](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1299360/Mighty_Goose/) (a shooter starring a bounty-hunter goose), [Untitled Goose Game](https://store.steampowered.com/app/837470/Untitled_Goose_Game/), and small itch.io goose games like [Be-Goose'd](https://rosscogames.itch.io/be-goosed) and [Goose Game](https://nejib.itch.io/goose-game).
- **Trademarks:** no GOOSE AT LARGE. The nearest marks:
  - [GOOSE](https://trademarks.justia.com/885/94/goose-88594816.html), Goose, LLC, Reg. 6139847. Per the search summary, it covers a band's recordings, merch and live shows: almost certainly the jam band Goose ([podcasts about the band](https://player.fm/podcasts/Goose-The-Band)).
  - [UNTITLED GOOSE GAME](https://trademarks.justia.com/793/28/untitled-goose-79328682.html), House House, for game software (per the search summary).
  - Famous goose marks in other fields: [GREY GOOSE](https://trademarks.justia.com/788/27/grey-goose-78827853.html) (Bacardi) and [CANADA GOOSE](https://trademarks.justia.com/859/70/canada-85970992.html).
  - A registered goose character: [GERTIE THE GOOD GOOSE](https://trademarks.justia.com/869/34/gertie-the-good-86934135.html), which is why Gertie is out as the goose's name.
- **The web:** nobody owns the phrase. Search engines read it as "large goose": [Maxie, the World's Largest Goose](https://www.roadsideamerica.com/story/7248), a statue in Missouri, and a [23-inch porch goose](https://www.mileskimball.com/buy-gaggleville-porch-goose-large-23-h-statue-310493). The phrase turns up in police news now and then: in July 2025 a runaway lizard named Goose was at large around Webster, Massachusetts ([Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2025/07/25/metro/lizard-runaway-goose-police-blotter-tales/), [NBC Boston](https://www.nbcboston.com/news/local/webster-elusive-water-monitor-captured/3782954/)).
- **Handles:** a web search for "gooseatlarge" found no site or account.

### Domains

Registrar check, 30 September 2026, with Vercel's prices:

- gooseatlarge.com: available, $11.25 a year.
- gooseatlarge.app: available, $9.99 the first year, then $15.
- gooseatlarge.game: available, but $349.99 the first year and $366.77 after. Skip it.

### Risks

- **"Large" can read as size.** Search engines already do it. The logo should lock "at large" together as one phrase (a rubber stamp on a wanted poster), and the subtitle should say what the game is.
- **It says the chase, not the finding.** The subtitle does the finding.
- **"Goose" is crowded in both stores** (Goose Goose Duck, Untitled Goose Game, Goosechase, apps called just Goose). What you own is the whole phrase, not the goose. A trademark examiner should see it the same way, but the USPTO search in the next steps is the real check.
- **The band Goose holds GOOSE** in the two classes you'll file in (9 and 41), for music. Different goods, so a low risk, but know it's there.
- **An unreleased app can hold a name** in App Store Connect without showing up in any search. Only reserving the name proves it's free.
- **Long names get cut off under the icon** on the home screen. That label is set separately from the store name, so check it on a phone and shorten it if it clips.

### App Store

- Name: **Goose at Large** (14 of 30 characters)
- Subtitle: **A hidden-object goose chase** (27 of 30). To lead with the places instead: **Find the goose in real places** (29).
- Keywords (100 characters, not shown to players): `seek and find,find it,search,spot,isometric,detective,mystery,whodunit,puzzle,real places,travel,map`. Leave out Waldo and other games' names: Apple's [guideline 2.3.7](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) forbids them, and words already in the name or subtitle don't need repeating.

### Does anything beat it?

No.

- **Goose of Interest** ties it on ownability and is better for the whodunits. But it's a pun, and a pun wears thinner than a plain phrase. It also says "suspect" rather than "out there somewhere", and it doesn't carry the travel.
- **Fugitive Goose** says the same thing as Goose at Large, less elegantly.
- **Have You Seen This Goose?** is the most instantly understood, but it fails your sentence test.

## The top 5 new names, ranked

### 1. Goose of Interest

**Why.** A "person of interest" is who the police want to question. Put a goose in it and you have the whole game. In every place the goose is the one everybody is looking for, and in the whodunits it's literally a suspect (it did it at the Manor). It's a pun, but an adult one. It's spelled the way it sounds, and the icon is a goose mugshot against a height chart. Even if you keep Goose at Large, it would make a good name for the whodunits or for one mystery place.

**Evidence**
- **App Store:** no match, only other goose apps: [Goose Goose Duck](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/goose-goose-duck/id1558248707), [Goosechase](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/goosechase/id437330879), [Tingus Goose](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tingus-goose-weird-idle-game/id6444581718), and an app called just [Goose](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/goose-for-the-boys/id6763519006).
- **Google Play:** no match: [I am Goose](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.tapempire.goosegame&hl=en_US), [Game of Goose](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=net.playtouch.gameofgoose&hl=en_US).
- **Steam and itch.io:** no match: [Untitled Goose Game](https://store.steampowered.com/app/837470/Untitled_Goose_Game/), [GOOSE.IO](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1176850/GOOSEIO/).
- **Trademarks:** no GOOSE OF INTEREST. Three searches found no PERSON OF INTEREST mark either; the nearest were [DEGREES OF INTEREST](https://www.trademarkia.com/degrees-of-interest-98691031) and Time's [PERSON OF THE YEAR](https://trademarks.justia.com/758/86/person-of-the-75886623.html). The goose marks nearby are the same as for Goose at Large.
- **The web:** nobody owns the phrase. Search splits it into "goose" and "interest": a game wiki's [Goose Lake](https://flockaround.wiki.gg/wiki/Goose_Lake) page (its "points of interest") and Canada Goose stock pages about [short interest](https://www.marketbeat.com/stocks/NYSE/GOOS/short-interest/).

**Domains:** gooseofinterest.com, .app and .game are all available.

**Risks**
- It says "suspect" more than "hidden". The subtitle has to say finding.
- It riffs on the TV series Person of Interest. That's fine for a pun; just don't borrow the show's look.
- Canada Goose stock news will crowd its search results.
- It's a syllable longer than Goose at Large when you say it.

**App Store:** **Goose of Interest** (17) with **Hidden-object goose mysteries** (29).

### 2. Fugitive Goose

**Why.** The chase in two words anyone understands. The posters are wanted posters, the goose is on the run from place to place, and Inspector Pidge, one place behind, is the marshal from The Fugitive. Not kiddy, and spelled the way it sounds.

**Evidence**
- **App Store:** no match. The nearest are [Escape Goose](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/escape-goose/id6742256131) and [Tap Tap Goose](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/goose-escape-endless-runner/id1538750194) (its web address still says goose-escape).
- **Google Play:** no match. There's a game called just [Fugitive](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.darkrockstudios.games.fugitive).
- **Steam and itch.io:** no match. itch.io has [Goose Chase](https://fienthedestroyer.itch.io/goose-chase) (per the search summary, a goose gang running from the police) and [FUGITIVE](https://glassgames.itch.io/fugitive).
- **Trademarks:** nothing close. The nearest are [FUGITIVE APPAREL CO.](https://www.trademarkia.com/fugitive-apparel-co-86649037) and [ANOTHER WILD GOOSE CHASE](https://www.trademarkia.com/another-wild-goose-chase-76323086).
- **The web:** nothing dominates. A 2013 web cartoon, [Space Goose](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/WebAnimation/SpaceGoose), called its first episode "Fugitive Goose" ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FhqnZa45wms)), and a tiny writing site calls itself [The fugitive goose](https://fugitivegoose.weebly.com/submit.html).

**Domains:** fugitivegoose.com and .app are available.

**Risks**
- It's close to a plain description of the game, and a plain description makes a weaker trademark than a twist on a phrase.
- It's Goose at Large said less elegantly. If you like it, you already have the better version.
- The small earlier uses above.

**App Store:** **Fugitive Goose** (14) with **Find the goose in real places** (29).

### 3. Have You Seen This Goose?

**Why.** It's your poster line, and it's the question the player answers in every place. Instantly understood, like Where's the Goose?, but it comes from missing-person posters rather than from Waldo.

**Evidence**
- **App Store, Google Play, Steam and itch.io:** no match for the full or the short form ("Seen This Goose?"). The goose results are the usual crowd ([Goose Goose Duck](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/goose-goose-duck/id1558248707), [The Goose Tale](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2773000/The_Goose_Tale/)), and the hidden-object shelf is full of "Found It" names instead ([example](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/found-it-hidden-object-game/id1643547847)).
- **Trademarks:** nothing for the phrase, only the general goose marks.
- **The web:** nobody owns it. Page one is a local TV station's "Have You Seen This?" viral-video segment, which happens to include goose clips ([KSL](https://www.ksl.com/article/44197232/have-you-seen-this-goose-gets-struck-by-lightning)). No posters, shirts or stickers use the phrase. The formula is familiar from Harry Potter's ["Have You Seen This Wizard?"](https://minalima.com/product/have-you-seen-this-wizard-poster/) poster, and Nancy Tafuri's picture book [Have You Seen My Duckling?](https://www.goodreads.com/en/book/show/2126734.Have_You_Seen_My_Duckling_) is a find-the-hidden-duckling book.

**Domains:** haveyouseenthisgoose.com and .app are available, and so is seenthisgoose.com.

**Risks**
- It fails your sentence test: "Have you played Have You Seen This Goose?"
- At 25 characters, the home-screen label would need a short form.
- A stock phrase with "goose" dropped in is harder to protect than a coined name.
- Its best use is as the tagline on the posters, the store screenshots and the trailer, under whatever name you pick.

**App Store:** **Have You Seen This Goose?** (25) with **Hidden object in real places** (28).

### 4. Gone Goose

**Why.** Short, dark and adult. "Gone" says missing, and "a gone goose" is an old idiom for someone past saving, which is the right kind of dark. At 10 characters it makes the best logo of the five.

**Evidence**
- **Stores:** no match anywhere. The nearest are a game-jam entry with the words flipped, [Goose a Gone](https://itch.io/jam/jame-gam-18/rate/1550934), and [Goose After Goose](https://sbstnc.itch.io/goose-after-goose) on itch.io. The App Store shows unrelated goose apps ([Expiry Goose](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/expiry-goose/id6757757797)).
- **Trademarks:** none. The nearest sound-alike is [GOO GONE](https://www.trademarkia.com/goo-gone-74623410), a cleaning product.
- **The web:** the idiom leads ([Merriam-Webster](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/gone%20goose)), then a nonfiction book, [Gone Goose: The Remaking of an American Town in the Age of Climate Change](https://www.barnesandnoble.com/w/gone-goose-braden-t-leap/1128591631), an electronic musician called [Gone Goose](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/gone-goose/961712752), and a [model plane plan](https://plans.modelaircraft.org/product/gone-goose/).

**Domains:** gonegoose.com, .app and .game are available.

**Risks**
- It will never own its search page: the idiom, the book and the musician got there first.
- It says nothing about looking or finding.
- It can read as "the goose is dead", which is darker than the game.

**App Store:** **Gone Goose** (10) with **A hidden-object goose chase** (27).

### 5. Goosespotting (provisional)

**Why.** The only name whose main word is what the player actually does. It nods to plane spotting (the airport level) and, for adults, to Trainspotting. One made-up word makes a clean wordmark.

**Evidence** (stores only: the trademark and web searches ran out)
- **App Store:** no match. The fuzzy search pairs it with [GSpot](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/gspot/id1142880104) and [GoSpotCheck](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/gospotcheck-team/id571011905); "Goose Spotting" lands among goose-hunting apps ([example](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/goose-hunting-calls/id1103210497)).
- **Google Play:** no match, just goose-hunting calls ([example](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.goose.calls&hl=en_US)).
- **Steam and itch.io:** no match. The nearest in spirit is a 2025 jam game, [Duck Duck Goose](https://itch.io/jam/fireside-jam-2025/rate/3511153), which per the search summary is about spotting a goose among ducks.

**Domains:** goosespotting.com, .app and .game are available.

**Risks**
- "Spotting" also means light bleeding, and the App Store's fuzzy search pairs it with an app called GSpot.
- Its store neighbors are goose-hunting apps.
- The trademark and web checks are still open, and you'd want both spellings (Goosespotting and Goose Spotting).
- Trainspotting is a heroin film: the nod lands for some people and not others.

**App Store:** **Goosespotting** (13) with **Spot the goose in real places** (29).

## The goose's name

**Recommendation: Geraldine.**

- **It passed every check.** No goose or bird called Geraldine turned up in books, cartoons, games, toys, apps or trademarks. The only goose Geraldine online is a story someone generated on an AI bedtime-story site ([link](https://www.bedtimestory.ai/brianfoody/story/lR1QPQB)).
- **A grand name for a small criminal.** "HAVE YOU SEEN THIS GOOSE? Answers to Geraldine."
- **It pays off the parcel.** "G. Goose" turns out to be Geraldine Goose.
- **No famous owner.** The best-known Geraldines are TV comedy characters, and neither is big enough to own the name: Flip Wilson's [Geraldine Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geraldine_Jones_(character)) and the [Vicar of Dibley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geraldine_Granger).
- **One decision comes with it.** The repo calls the goose "it" (the Manor brief: "It swapped his heart pills for mints"). Geraldine makes it a she. If you'd rather keep "it", Gene works either way.

## Next steps for you

### 1. Register the domains (about $21, ten minutes)

- Buy **gooseatlarge.com** ($11.25 a year) and **gooseatlarge.app** ($9.99 the first year, then $15) at Vercel. ryankm.com already lives there, so pointing them at the game is one click. .app only works over HTTPS, which Vercel handles for you.
- Skip gooseatlarge.game ($349.99).
- Optional: gooseofinterest.com ($11) to keep that name in reserve for the whodunits.
- Do this first: it's the cheapest step and the quickest to lose.

### 2. Search the USPTO (half an hour)

- Go to [tmsearch.uspto.gov](https://tmsearch.uspto.gov). Search `goose at large`, then `at large` on its own, then `goose`. Filter to live marks and to classes 9 and 41.
- Class 9 covers downloadable games and apps. Class 41 covers online games and entertainment.
- You're looking for any live mark for games, apps or entertainment that a player could confuse with Goose at Large. From this research, expect these three, none of them "at large":
  - GOOSE (the band: music, in classes 9 and 41)
  - UNTITLED GOOSE GAME (game software)
  - famous goose marks in other fields (GREY GOOSE, CANADA GOOSE)
- **If it's clear, file in classes 9 and 41.**
  - The USPTO's base fee is $350 per class, so $700 for both ([USPTO fees](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/trademark-fee-information)).
  - It costs more if you write the description of goods yourself instead of picking it from the USPTO's list ([2025 fee changes](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/fees-payment-information/summary-2025-trademark-fee-changes)).
  - You can file before launch, as "intent to use".
- **A trademark attorney's clearance search is worth paying for before you pay for a logo.** It also catches state marks and unregistered uses that a database search misses.
- **Later, for merch:** class 25 (clothing), 16 (posters and prints) and 28 (toys and board games).

### 3. Reserve the name in App Store Connect (an evening)

- You need the Apple Developer Program ($99 a year).
- In App Store Connect, go to Apps, press +, then New App. Pick iOS, type **Goose at Large**, and choose a bundle ID and a SKU ([Apple's steps](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/create-an-app-record/add-a-new-app/)).
- Creating the app record claims the name. App names must be unique on the App Store, so nobody else can use it while you hold it.
- Apple used to release a name after 180 days without an upload. Developers report that the limit is gone ([forum thread](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/706597), [blog post](https://paavandesign.com/blog/reserve-the-name-of-your-app-on-the-ios-app-store)), but don't lean on it: upload a TestFlight build when you can.
- The name and the subtitle can each be 30 characters, and the keywords 100 ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page/)).

### 4. Claim the social handles (an hour)

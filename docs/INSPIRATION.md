# Inspiration

Reference images the owner has collected for future places, and what each one teaches. The images themselves are other artists' work, so they are **not** stored in the repo; this file keeps the notes and the credit. Sent in batches of five; add each batch as a new section.

When a new place is planned, start here: pick the lessons it takes, and write them into its map brief (see ROADMAP.md, "How a new place gets made").

## The six things every great one has

Every strong reference so far has all six. Every new place should too.

1. **A silhouette.** You could recognize it from its outline alone. Not a square.
2. **A hero landmark.** One thing bigger and stranger than everything else (a waterfall, a giant tub, a windmill tower, a chasm).
3. **Connections.** Bridges, stairs, lifts, rivers, paths. People move between areas; the place feels like one world, not separate boxes.
4. **Its own plate.** A palette and print feel that belongs to it (full color, one ink, soft watercolor edges).
5. **Its own cast.** Who lives there changes with the place (beachgoers, rats in a bathhouse, sea monsters, dragons).
6. **A running story per area.** Every corner has a small joke playing out (the free sofa, the pigeons on the taxi).

A seventh, ours to add: **one mechanic that belongs only to that place** (see ROADMAP.md).

## Batch 1

### 1. Island festival (Jesse Martin, @jesse_martin_)
Top-down oblique view of an island ring around a lagoon.
- **Silhouette:** organic coastline, a C-shaped ring of land around turquoise water, sea all around.
- **Hero:** a waterfall pouring from a cliff-top pool into the lagoon.
- **Structure:** land in tiers (beach, then grassy plateaus on brown cliffs). Rope bridges and a boardwalk join the tiers.
- **Density:** a huge beach crowd, hundreds of people, and it still reads because the sand is plain and bright.
- **Edges:** boats, swimmers, rocks and rubber rings scattered in the sea around it, so the edge of the map is alive too.
- **Lesson:** terrain with height and water is the single biggest thing our engine lacks.

### 2. Park and street (David Regone, @david_regone)
A tight isometric crop of a park corner meeting a New York street.
- **Scale:** big characters, about a dozen, each a clear, weird little story: someone asleep on a "FREE BED" mattress, a "FREE SOFA" with a toaster and lamp on it, pigeons all over a taxi, a man on a ride-on mower in traffic, a painter at an easel, a cop writing a ticket.
- **Plate:** thick outlines, flat color, very close to our style but bigger.
- **Lesson:** a place doesn't have to be a big map. A close, dense crop with bigger people is a different kind of level (fewer finds, funnier ones). Needs character scale per place.

### 3. The chasm (black and white ink, artist unknown)
A vertical cross-section of the earth: a dark chasm splits the page, and rooms, houses, arches and caves are carved into the rock on both sides at every depth.
- **Silhouette:** tall and narrow. A grassy surface at the top, then down and down.
- **Hero:** the chasm itself, with people and objects falling through it.
- **Connections:** stairs everywhere, bridges across the void, ladders.
- **Plate:** a single ink, gray washes. Proof that one ink can be richer than six.
- **Cast:** people mixed with monsters, goblins, dragons, skeletons in the rock.
- **Lesson:** a vertical place made for scrolling (perfect on phones), rooms carved into terrain rather than boxes on a plate, and a one-ink plate.

### 4. Rat bathhouse town (signed "Wizard Fuzzy Gerbil")
A floating isometric diorama: a Japanese bathhouse town run by rats.
- **Hero:** an enormous wooden tub, full of bathing rats, fed by a bamboo pipe from a mountain with a torii gate.
- **Edges:** the island sits on water that fades softly into white paper. No hard frame.
- **Plate:** warm watercolor, soft, lower contrast than ours.
- **Cast:** all rats.
- **Structure:** one hero object, a crowded festival square, a cherry tree branching over everything, a sake barrel, a pagoda.
- **Lesson:** a place can be one giant object plus a crowd around it. A cast that isn't people is a big creative jump.

### 5. Fantasy island tower (signature unreadable)
A tall rocky island rising out of the sea: a windmill tower, a cutaway house, a sleeping dragon, caves inside the rock, a cable lift running from the clifftop down to a harbor.
- **Silhouette:** vertical, sky at the top (a flying whale, clouds), sea at the bottom (sea serpents, fish, a turtle).
- **Hero:** the cable lift and the windmill.
- **Structure:** cutaway rooms inside the rock and inside buildings, stairs winding up the outside.
- **Lesson:** a vertical journey from sea to sky, with a machine that carries you through it. Mixing outdoor terrain with cutaway interiors.

## How this batch changes the plan

- The Walk-Up is too close to The Block (both are square dollhouse rooms). Future places should break the square.
- The engine needs: terrain and water, zones that are invisible gameplay areas rather than drawn boxes, per-place palettes, per-place casts, character scale, and soft map edges. All in ROADMAP.md.

## In one line
Lord Gooseworth's 80th birthday dinner, a stormy night, a body in the library, and six guests who each had a very good reason.

## The story
**The setup.** Lord Barnaby Gooseworth, taxidermist, tyrant and terrible host, is found dead in his library at midnight, face down in the birthday trifle. The storm has cut the road. Nobody can leave. Inspector Pidge has arrived (his car is stuck in the drive) and has already arrested the butler.

**What the player does.** Every room holds evidence. Find it all, then make an accusation from the suspects' portraits. A wrong accusation gets that suspect's alibi (and a joke). The right one plays the reveal.

**The suspects** (each with a motive the evidence first seems to confirm, and an alibi another find later reveals):

| Suspect | Motive | Looks guilty because | Cleared by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Lady Philippa**, the much younger wife | The inheritance | Her lipstick on a wine glass in the library | Love letters in the conservatory: she was out there with the gardener all night |
| **Rupert**, the son | Gambling debts | The family silver, half-packed in his room | The pawn ticket is dated tomorrow; he was in the billiard room losing again |
| **Dr. Mortimer Crane**, the family doctor | Named in the new will | An empty bottle of heart pills in his bag | The pills were swapped for mints; his bag is missing the mints, not the pills |
| **Mrs. Hatchett**, the cook | Forty years of insults about her trifle | Her rolling pin, found in the hall | Flour prints show she was baking the replacement trifle at midnight |
| **Brigadier Snort**, old friend | A debt from 1974 | A duelling pistol, cleaned | It hasn't been fired since 1974 (a dusty cork is still in the barrel) |
| **Jenkins**, the butler | Obviously | He's packing a suitcase; Pidge arrested him | His resignation letter is dated last week |

**The solution.** The goose did it. Lord Gooseworth had ordered a new display stand for his taxidermy room, labelled "GOOSE". The goose read the order form. It swapped his heart pills for mints, then waited. The evidence: goose feathers on the library rug, webbed footprints through the kitchen flour, beak marks on the pill bottle cap, the taxidermy order form ("1 x goose, stuffed, by Tuesday"), and the empty display stand. Accusing the goose plays the reveal: the goose, in the Lord's chair, wearing his monocle.

## Shape
**One big house at night in a storm**, set in its grounds. Nothing like the Block: one building whose rooms connect through doors, with gardens around it and weather over it.
- **Storeys:** cellar, ground floor and upstairs, shown one storey at a time (like a floor plan you flip through), with the others faded. The overview shows the whole house with the roof lifted off.
- **Walls down:** looking at the whole house, inside walls are cut to waist height (like The Sims) so you see into every room. Step into a room and its walls rise to full height.
- **The grounds:** a hedge maze, the family crypt, a greenhouse and the drive, around the house in the rain.
- **Silhouette:** a crooked Gothic pile with chimneys and a tower, lit windows against a navy storm.
- **Hero:** the library, two storeys tall, with the body at its center and a stuffed bear towering over it.

## Cast
- **The six suspects** above, plus the **gardener**, the **chauffeur**, a **maid** who has seen everything and says nothing, **the dog**, and **Inspector Pidge** (a pigeon in a trench coat, recurring across places, always wrong).
- **The late Lord Gooseworth**, face down in trifle. Present in spirit (a ghost appears in lightning flashes, pointing furiously at the goose; nobody notices).
- **The goose**, playing innocent throughout.

## Palette and plate
Night: deep navy exterior with rain; rooms lit warm from inside (lamps, candles, fire). Draft inks: storm navy `#1F2749`, deep plum `#4B2E4F`, oxblood `#8E2F3A`, candle gold `#E8B04A`, verdigris `#4E8A7C`, bone `#EFE3C8`. Paper `#E9DCC4`. Lightning flashes briefly light everything cold white.

## Alive
- **The storm:** rain on the grounds and windows, trees thrashing, lightning every 15 to 25 seconds with thunder.
- **Guests on a loop:** everyone moves between rooms through doors on a 3-minute loop of the evening (the toast, the lights going out, the scream, everyone running to the library). A guest walks out one door and in through the next.
- **Light:** candles flicker, the fire crackles, the lights flicker and cut out during the "lights out" part of the loop.
- **Sound bed:** rain, thunder, a grandfather clock, distant organ.

## Mechanic
**Lightning shows the past.** On every flash, faint silhouettes appear for a moment showing where everyone was at midnight. Watch enough flashes and you'll see a small, goose-shaped silhouette in the library.

Evidence (all needed to accuse), across the rooms:

Plus just-for-fun finds, one or two per room (a false tooth in the soup, a skeleton in the cupboard, literally).

The tray groups finds as **Evidence** and **Curiosities**. The goose is a find too: it's always somewhere in the house, looking innocent.

## Shared universe
- **The goose:** the culprit.
- **Inspector Pidge:** first appearance. He'll turn up in later places, still on the goose's trail.
- **The Courier:** a soaked parcel on the doorstep for "G. Goose" (it's the monocle).
- **"Have you seen this goose?"** poster: in the servants' corridor.

## Tone
Dark comedy: a body in a trifle, taxidermy, a ghost, poison. No gore; death is played for laughs. Adult jokes welcome in the margins (the conservatory, the cellar).

## Session 3: the art and the case

**How it plays.** Every find the case file names is **evidence** (17, marked with a magnifying glass); the rest are **curiosities** (9), plus the goose by the coats in the hall. The **Case** button counts evidence and opens the case file: seven portrait cards (the six suspects and "Someone else?"). Tap one for their motive, what points at them and their alibi, and Accuse. A wrong accusation plays as a conversation with Inspector Pidge (the alibi, and a joke) and stamps them Cleared; finding their alibi clears them too. "Someone else?" is a plain shadow with a question mark ("Not on the guest list") until all six clues against the goose are found (Library 2, Kitchen 1, Taxidermy Room 2, Master Bedroom 1); naming it before then gets "I can't arrest a question mark." Naming the goose: Pidge's summing up, GUILTY, a flash of lightning, and the camera cuts to dinner, where the goose sits in the Lord's chair wearing the monocle from the Courier's parcel. Then "Case closed".

**The mechanic.** Every lightning flash shows dark shapes of where everyone stood at midnight (88 s), each in the room they were in, and a goose-shaped one by the body in the library.

**Sound.** Rain all night; thunder after each strike (sooner and louder for the big ones); a distant organ at 57 s; the lights' clunk at 82; the grandfather clock striking midnight; a stamp and a sting for the verdict.

**The art.** Eleven rooms and the outside, drawn by parallel agents, then one art direction pass (see "Decisions" 11 to 20). Everything draws from `ROOM` and `MAT` in the style sheet.

## Decisions
Brief approved by the owner. The three open questions were settled with the lead's recommendations as defaults (the owner can still change them at the greybox gate):
1. **Name:** Gooseworth Manor.
2. **The goose is the killer** in this first whodunit. Later mysteries need a different culprit so it doesn't get predictable.
3. **Accuse any time.** A wrong accusation plays that suspect's alibi and a joke; that's where a lot of the humor lives.

Greybox choices (session 2), for the owner to confirm or change at gate 2:
4. **It opens on the ground floor**, with the upstairs lifted off as a faint ghost, because that's where the crime scene and six of the eleven areas are. The floor switch shows the whole house (Upstairs) or the cellar.
5. **The grounds are one area, in front of the house:** the drive, Pidge's car, a very small maze, the crypt and the front door (whose wall rises when you step outside). The greenhouse and the rest of the lawn are scenery around it. Wrapping the grounds all the way round the house needs areas of any shape (E5); the alternative now is a second outdoor area.
6. **No roof.** The silhouette comes from the tower, the chimneys and the trees rather than a roof floating over the house.
7. **The cellar is always visible** in the cut through the ground, as well as from the floor switch.
8. **Guest Rooms** holds the Brigadier's and Dr. Crane's rooms and the corridor; Rupert's half-packed silver is in the corridor, not in a room of his own.
9. **Outdoors, the house stays whole:** stepping into the grounds lifts nothing, so the upstairs shows over the front wall.
10. **Tagline:** "A stormy night, a body in the trifle, six suspects and a goose."

Owner approved the greybox (gate 2) at the start of session 3.

Session 3 choices, for the owner to confirm or change at gate 3:
11. **Accuse any time, but the culprit needs the evidence.** Wrong accusations are free and play the alibi; the goose stays "Someone else?" until all six clues against it are found. That keeps "accuse any time" and "evidence needed" both true.

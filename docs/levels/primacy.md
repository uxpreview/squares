## What's known

Facts, with where they came from. Guesses are marked.

- **The firm.** Primacy, founded 1994 as Acsys Interactive by Stan Valencis. Renamed Primacy around 2012 and moved here from 6 Executive Drive, Farmington, doubling its space; about 80 people then, 120+ later across Farmington, Boston and West Palm Beach. The building was retrofitted to LEED standards (Hartford Business, read only as a summary; the article returned 403). Primacy's site now calls the office "Hartford, CT"; it may have moved or gone mostly remote.
- **The property.** Called the Ecos Center. 4.49 acres, zoned CR, office. A LoopNet sale listing called it about 25,000 sq ft in "three separate buildings" with older roof and siding; RealtyTrac says built 1988, 2 storeys, 13,143 sq ft (likely one record of several). ECOS Building LLC and Ecos LLC (a green builder, Suite 4) share the address; probably the landlord, unconfirmed.
- **The site** (OpenStreetMap, `primacy/osm-site.json`). One building tagged "Primacy, Inc.", 2 levels, about 930 m² a floor. A loop drive off New Britain Avenue with the parking inside it. Shade Swamp Sanctuary (state land, white- and blue-blazed trails) wraps the back, with a path starting at the south edge. The Farmington Canal Heritage Trail (the bike trail) is about 230 m west, across the road.
- **The aerial photo** (the owner's, golden hour, summer; not stored in the repo). It's turned with west at the top: the road along the top, the woods at the bottom. The OSM outline lands right on the building (`primacy/photo-fit.json` maps one onto the other, about 14.9 pixels a metre). What it shows:
  - Five or six linked pieces under hipped asphalt shingle roofs, plus an **octagon** and a small six-sided **turret** in the middle (`primacy/roofs.json`, traced in photo pixels).
  - Four skylights on the wing by the octagon, one on the west wing.
  - The front walk leaves the long sidewalk for a small plaza with a round planter and leads to the octagon.
  - A small gabled porch on the middle wing, facing the sidewalk.
  - The **fire pit** on a curved patio in the crook of the building, on the woods side.
  - A back deck with steps off the west wing; the drive carries on round the back of it.
  - Air conditioners boxed in at the end of the far wing; a row of blue-green panels in the gravel (unknown: benches? window wells?).
  - A shed at the edge of the woods; a dumpster pen at the far side of the lot; about 100 parking spaces with tree islands; a lawn and big trees along the road.
- **The flyover** (Apple Maps 3D, the owner's screen recording; not stored).
  - **The building is dug into a slope.** From the lot it's two storeys, the top one under the roof with the skylights. From the woods it's three: a white wall of windows stepping in and out, the patio, the fire pit and a door at the bottom. So there's a lower floor that walks out to the woods, and about 930 m² over two and a half floors is about 25,000 sq ft, which matches the listing.
  - Glass doors at the foot of the octagon: **the front door**.
  - The west end has stacked bays and balconies over the back drive.
  - The front read dark slate in the flyover but was in shade; its real colour is unknown.

## Guesses on the page (to be corrected)

- **Lower floor:** kitchen and café opening onto the patio and fire pit; restrooms; a training room; the front half dug into the hill (storage, servers).
- **Main floor** (level with the lot): lobby and reception in the octagon, a stair in the turret, the big meeting room (the pitch room) right off the lobby, studios in the wings, a side door at the porch.
- **Upper floor:** desks under the skylights, a creative studio, offices and a boardroom at the quiet far end.

## Open questions for the owner

1. The lower floor: what was down there? Was the kitchen by the patio?
2. The octagon and the turret: what's inside? Is the octagon open to the floor above?
3. The front: what colour is the siding?
4. The balconies and the back deck: could you walk out onto them?
5. Who sat where: which teams, which wing, which floor?
6. The rooms with names, and the door signage (the one Foursquare tip says "Be sure to see all the door signage").
7. The blue-green panels and the shed.
8. Which years: the place should be set in the owner's years there.
9. People: real coworkers in a game need their say-so. Made-up names and recognisable types unless they've agreed. Public, or shared privately with old colleagues?
10. The day it all happens on, which should lead to the verb (a pitch, a launch, a Friday fire pit, a snowstorm, the holiday party).

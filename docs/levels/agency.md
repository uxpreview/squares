## In one line
A digital agency on the day of the big pitch: seven geese loose on the top floor, everyone pretending to work, and a bear in the parking lot that nobody can stop looking at.

## The story
A light one, like Departures': it runs across the map and never gets in the way of the geese. The goal is every goose (one per area) and most of the things.

**The setup.** Primal is a digital agency on the top floor of an office building in the woods, somewhere in New England, in another universe (decision 2). Floors one and two have been empty since the reorg. Today is Friday, the last Friday in October, and at four o'clock Primal pitches **Gander Cola** for its whole account. Creative needed a goose for the mascot shoot and booked one from a casting agency. The casting agency sent seven. They got loose at nine.

**What the player does.** Find the goose and the things in each area, as everywhere. And **wait for bear o'clock** (the verb, below): three times a day a black bear wanders out of the woods into the lot, someone shouts, and the whole floor runs to the windows. For a minute every desk is left alone, and what people keep hidden is out where anyone can see it.

**The day.** People arrive from half past seven, the coffee machine is broken by nine (QA), stand-ups at half past nine, the CEO walks the floor twice (every screen she passes flips to a spreadsheet), the bear at quarter past ten (the dumpster), one o'clock (a man eating lunch in his car) and half past four (on the CEO's car, during the pitch). The pitch room is set up all day and the screen says NO SIGNAL until two minutes to four. At twenty past five it's Friday: everyone goes down to the fire pit and the floor empties, except Dev, who are deploying on a Friday despite the sign. The cleaner at nine. The bear has the lot to itself all night.

**The Courier** has a parcel for "G. Goose, c/o Creative". He arrives at nine and waits at reception all day, where the sign says BACK IN 5 MINUTES. Nobody comes. At five he signs for it himself.

**Inspector Pidge** is participant 4 in UX's usability lab, behind the one-way mirror, and has been trying to find the Buy button since nine.

**The ending.** Find every goose and most of the things and the clock jumps to half past four: the bear is sitting on the hood of the CEO's car, right under the pitch room's window. The Gander Cola people have stopped listening and gone to the window with everyone else. The camera flies there. The seven geese come out of the front door in a line, walk across the lot past the bear (who lets them) and into the woods. In the pitch room the Gander Cola client turns round and says "We love the goose."

## Mechanic
**Bear o'clock: catch them off-task.** No other place has it (decision 5).

- **Three times a day the bear comes out of the woods** into the lot (10:15, 1pm, 4:20). Someone sees it, a notification pings round the floor, and **everyone runs to the front windows.** For a minute or so every desk is left alone.
- **What people hide is out in the open while they're gone:** the screens they'd minimize, the snack drawer, what's on their chair. Each stretch of the floor has **one find that's only there at bear o'clock** (`when`, with the note "bear o'clock"), so the list says "(at bear o'clock)" and the toast says so if you tap too early.
- **The bear watch** is the place's dial (`map.dial`, as Plum Island's tide): it says where the bear is ("All quiet in the lot", "Something in the woods", "BEAR IN THE LOT"), its gauge fills as the bear gets close, and a tap skips to the next bear o'clock. Nothing new in the engine: it's the tide's dial with a bear in it.
- **The CEO's walks** are the other half of the rhythm, for the eye rather than the list: as she passes, every screen near her flips from what its owner was really doing to a spreadsheet, and flips back once she's gone. It teaches the floor's rule (screens are worth looking at) without being a rule.
- Why it isn't the tide again (the owner's test, "verb first"): the tide moves the ground; here the **people** move. The place's whole crowd gets up and goes to one side, which no other place does, and what you find is what they left behind.
